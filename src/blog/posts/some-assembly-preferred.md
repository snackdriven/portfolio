<p class="lead">I saw a version online with room for nine albums and laughed out loud. Someone could have only nine albums that shaped them?</p>

I like a weird amount of music, and I&rsquo;ve been making monthly Spotify playlists since 2019. Nine slots felt optimistic.

I liked the idea enough to want my own version. I like building stuff, and sometimes that means taking an idea I have seen somewhere else and finding the version I want to use.

The [album poster builder](https://albums.snackdriven.com/) lets you pick nine, twelve, or fifteen records, move the covers around, change the background, and export a poster. There are captions and a name subtitle. You can bring your own cover art. I wanted room to keep adjusting the collection until it looked like mine.

The [film version](https://films.snackdriven.com/) gives movies the same treatment. Both keep the board in your browser, with backups and share links. The links leave uploaded images out; those stay with the browser and backup. A little poster maker still has decisions about what belongs in a shared copy.

That is the part I enjoy: following an idea into its details. Choosing what someone can change, then checking whether the controls behave the way they suggest. The film search needed to understand that *1917* is a title, for example. Four digits were doing more work than expected.

AI writes the code. I set the direction, work through the choices, test the result, and ask for revisions. Being able to try a version makes the next decision easier. Sometimes the mockup settles it. Sometimes using the thing exposes an entirely different problem.

The keyboard controls and movie brackets come from the same interest. I like getting a say in how a thing works and what it feels like. A small tool can be useful and still have room for taste, including mine.

Nine choices was never going to be enough.
