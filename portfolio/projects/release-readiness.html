01Problem

A ticket status, a merged pull request, and the build running on staging could each tell a different story. Late changes meant checking the release candidate again. Delivery needed a clear account of what had actually reached production.

My responsibility continued through the handoff: reconcile the scope, prepare the candidate, check readiness, and communicate the current state.

02Decisions

I checked ticket scope against merged code and release tags. I kept staging, demo, and production status separate, repeated smoke checks when a candidate changed, and coordinated production steps with infrastructure colleagues.

In July, apparent last-minute feature-flag changes prompted another scope check. Checking the merge timing showed the changes were already included. I also raised the cost of repeated late cuts and worked with a colleague on clearer cutoff and release-planning steps.

My personal reporting tools assembled Jira, GitHub, and release context into working documents. AI helped with implementation and draft preparation; I reviewed the evidence, corrected the scope, and controlled what I posted. The QA workflow case covers those review steps.

04Team use

The July release conversation explicitly relied on me making the cut and acknowledged my work on release hygiene. In October, delivery replied to my production-release thread to say it would tell the customer that production had been updated.

Those are concrete uses of the work: engineering coordinated around the release candidate, and delivery used the production status for its next communication.
