Professional QA investigation

Making a platform-wide QA investigation usable

I directed and checked an AI-assisted staging investigation, then organized the findings around recurring patterns, data risks, and unresolved decisions.

Scope
Four-day staging pass; one QA account, Chromium, seeded data
My role
Test strategy, investigation direction, evidence review, reporting
Findings
250 documented findings in 23 recurring patterns
Handoff
Five decisions routed to product and engineering owners

Contents

ProblemDecisionsValidationEvidenceLimits

Problem

A new platform had reached staging before launch decisions. An undifferentiated issue list would not tell readers which observations threatened data, which appeared to share a cause, or where intended behavior still needed a product decision.

The investigation needed a readout that made those relationships visible, along with the limits of what had actually been tested.

Decisions and implementation

I organized the pass across application sections, administration settings, and cross-page behavior. I directed AI-assisted browser harnesses and checked their work. The investigation looked beyond an initial save to refresh, stale forms, repeated actions, and state changes elsewhere.

I grouped the 250 documented findings into 23 patterns and prioritized data risks for the handoff. The readout separated observed behavior from questions about intended behavior. Five unresolved decisions went to product and engineering owners.

Validation and correction

I used repeat checks and a second review that tried to disprove higher-impact findings. Some claims changed: configuration checks led to withdrawals, and one result moved to a flaky classification when a second run did not establish it. A persistence claim required a real save and readback before it could be stated as stored data.

The footage-to-description audit kept full matches separate from partial support and steps the recording did not show. A clip showing a Save click, for example, would not by itself prove persistence after reload.

Evidence and results

The four-day pass produced 250 documented findings organized into 23 patterns. The readout highlighted 22 data risks covering 53 flagged issues and routed five unresolved decisions. It gave readers a prioritized summary with detailed evidence behind it.

The readout was circulated to engineering leadership. This case ends at investigation and handoff; it does not claim completed fixes.

Limits and next pass

Coverage used one QA account, Chromium, and seeded staging data. Two windows on one account did not establish cross-user behavior. The work did not establish cross-device behavior or full platform coverage.

A next pass could turn the recurring patterns into repeatable probes, include deferred coverage, and identify decision owners before testing begins. The documented findings guide that work; they do not replace it.
