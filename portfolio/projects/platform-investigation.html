Professional QA investigation

Making a platform-wide QA investigation usable

A platform-wide investigation needs a useful ending. I directed an AI-assisted staging pass, reviewed the evidence, and turned a large set of findings into patterns, data risks, and decisions for product and engineering.

Scope
Four-day staging pass; one QA account, Chromium, seeded data
My role
Test strategy, investigation direction, evidence review, reporting
Findings
250 documented findings in 23 recurring patterns
Handoff
Five decisions routed to product and engineering owners

Contents

ProblemDecisionsValidationEvidenceLimits

Problem

A new platform had reached staging ahead of launch decisions. The team needed to know which observations threatened data, which appeared connected, and which depended on a product decision about intended behavior. A long list of issues would leave that sorting work to the reader.

My job was to make the investigation usable: show the relationships, prioritize the risks, and be clear about where coverage stopped.

Decisions and implementation

I organized the pass across application sections, administration settings, and cross-page behavior. I directed AI-assisted browser harnesses and checked their work. A Save click was the beginning of the check: refresh, stale forms, repeated actions, and changes elsewhere could tell a different story.

I grouped the 250 documented findings into 23 patterns and prioritized data risks for the handoff. The readout separated observed behavior from questions about intended behavior. Five unresolved decisions went to product and engineering owners.

Validation and correction

I repeated checks and used a second review to try to disprove the higher-impact findings. Configuration checks led to withdrawals, and one result became flaky when another run did not establish it. A claim about stored data needed an actual save and readback. Correcting a finding was part of the investigation.

The footage audit separated full matches, partial support, and steps the recording never showed. A video of a Save click proves the click. A persistence claim still needs the reload and readback.

Evidence and results

The four-day pass produced 250 documented findings organized into 23 patterns. The readout highlighted 22 data risks covering 53 flagged issues and routed five unresolved decisions. It gave readers a prioritized summary with detailed evidence behind it.

The readout was circulated to engineering leadership. The result documented here is the investigation and handoff; completed fixes remain outside this case study.

Limits and next pass

Coverage used one QA account, Chromium, and seeded staging data. Two windows on one account did not establish cross-user behavior. The work did not establish cross-device behavior or full platform coverage.

The next useful move would be to turn those recurring patterns into repeatable probes, include the deferred coverage, and identify decision owners earlier. The findings give that pass a starting point, with the unfinished work still visible.
