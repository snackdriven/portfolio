Felt Presence: shaping a story reader

I shaped an illustrated reader for a fictional haunted craft shop, then connected recurring AI-assisted stories and illustrations to automatic publishing.

Result: A public reader that publishes complete, accepted story/image pairs.

Problem

The page needed to feel handmade, seasonal, and a little odd. The unopened letter and illustration also needed room, without an unnecessary scroll.

Decisions and implementation

I chose the name and recurring format, shaped character and visual references, and reviewed AI-generated stories and illustrations for continuity.

I directed AI-assisted implementation of autumn stamps, colors, and vines. The first vines were too tidy. I asked for looser growth, softer transparency, and a lower-corner curl.

I consolidated repeated timestamps and removed extra scrolling from the unopened view. Decoration had to leave the letter readable.

Keeping the shop stocked

I configured recurring AI-assisted story and illustration generation around the archive’s continuity and visual references. I connected that workflow to publication: once a complete story and accepted image are archived and pushed, Cloudflare rebuilds the Eleventy reader. Each new letter gets a place in the shop without a separate manual publishing step.

Validation and refinement

I checked the whole experience: the unopened page, the moment of opening, and the letter itself. The vines needed enough presence to feel intentional while leaving the story in charge. That review caught the extra scroll and competing date and time labels.

After shipping, I approved scaling the unopened illustration to available height. The October 15, 2026 record shows document height changing from 1,298px to 900px at 1600 × 900, and from 632px to 568px at 320 × 568. The full illustration stayed visible; open letters and very short screens kept scrolling. These are historical measurements.

Limits and lessons

The puppet theatre is a portfolio illustration, not a reader screenshot or story installment. Source and art references remain private. I owned creative direction, requirements, and review; implementation was AI-assisted.

A vine, a timestamp, an awkward scroll: small decisions change how a letter reads.
