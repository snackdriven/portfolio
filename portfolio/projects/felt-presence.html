Personal creative project

Felt Presence: shaping a story reader

I directed the experience of an illustrated letter reader for a fictional haunted craft shop, then refined its visual details and page behavior through review and AI-assisted implementation.

Context
Personal story and reader project
My role
Creative direction, experience requirements, implementation direction, review
Focus
Seasonal atmosphere, layout, and reading flow
Status
Public reader; private source and story archive

Contents

ProblemDecisionsValidationEvidenceLimits

Problem

A letter from a tiny haunted craft shop needed a place to be read. I wanted the reader to carry the shop's handmade, seasonal character without making the page harder to use. The unopened letter and its illustration needed room to breathe, and the page needed to fit before the reader opened the letter.

Decisions and implementation

I set the experience requirements and directed AI-assisted implementation. For the autumn version, I asked for stamps, vines across the blank edges, more transparency, and fall colors. The first vine treatment felt too symmetrical and too confined to the corners, so I asked for looser growth, including a curl around a lower corner.

I also wanted the date and time to have one clear home. Repeated labels competed with the letter, so I consolidated them and removed unwanted scrolling from the unopened view. These were choices about what a reader sees first and how easily they can reach the story.

Validation and refinement

I reviewed the page as an experience rather than treating each visual element as a separate deliverable. The vines needed to feel present across the empty space, but the letter still had to remain the focus. I checked the layout before opening a letter, caught the extra scroll, and asked for the date and time to be consolidated.

The result came from a sequence of specific requests, review, and correction. The check was whether the reader felt coherent and usable, not whether a first generated version looked decorative.

Evidence and result

The public reader presents illustrated letters from the fictional shop. It provides a direct way to see the reading experience described here: visit Felt Presence.

My documented decisions include the seasonal treatment, stamps, vine placement and transparency, less symmetrical composition, removal of the unwanted initial scroll, and consolidation of date and time.

Limits and lessons

The source archive and original art references are private. This case uses a labeled diagram instead of copying story assets or presenting a reconstruction as an actual screen. My contribution is creative direction, requirements, implementation guidance, and iterative review. It does not imply that I hand-coded the reader or hold a professional design credential.

The lesson I keep from this work is practical: atmosphere lives in small decisions, and those decisions have to survive the actual reading flow.
